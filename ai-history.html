A History of AI · 1950 — 2026
The Evolution of

Artificial Intelligence

From the Turing Test to Agentic AI  ·  1950–2026

Seventy-six years in one scroll — the ideas, the breakthroughs, and the two long winters that shaped how machines learned to think. Warm markers are springs of progress; cool markers are the AI winters when the funding and the faith ran out.

1950 – 1959

The Birth of Artificial Intelligence

1950 Foundational idea

The Turing Test

Alan Turing — the codebreaker who cracked Nazi Enigma — asks in "Computing Machinery and Intelligence": can a machine's conversation be indistinguishable from a human's? His "Imitation Game" set AI's goalpost for the next 25 years.

1957 – 1958 Learning machine

The Perceptron

Frank Rosenblatt at Cornell builds a Navy-funded physical machine with 400 photocells as a "retina." It learned to classify patterns by trial and error — no hard-coded rules. The press hype about conscious machines proves today's AI hype isn't new.

1959 Term coined

"Machine Learning" is named

Arthur Samuel names programs that improve through experience — learning from data instead of being explicitly told what to do.

1960s – early 1980s

Early Progress & the First AI Winter

1960s Boom

Rapid early progress

AI research boomed. Programs solved math and logic; expert systems mimicked human experts in medicine and engineering; games and language translation advanced — and governments and companies poured in funding.

mid-1970s First AI Winter

Why the winter came

Computers were too slow, digital data too scarce, and expectations too high. Real-world tasks defeated the systems, disappointment set in, funding was cut, and projects stopped — the First AI Winter.

The lasting lesson Insight

Stumble before success

New technologies often stumble before they succeed. Researchers kept going; faster computers, better algorithms and more data eventually revived the field.

1974 – 1987

The Winter Deepens, Then the Expert-Systems Boom

1974 – 1980 First Winter continues

The Lighthill Report

The 1973 Lighthill Report declares AI has failed its "grandiose objectives." UK funding collapses almost overnight; DARPA quietly scales back in the US. "Artificial intelligence" becomes a term researchers avoid in grant proposals — work continues under quieter names like pattern recognition and heuristic programming.

1980 – 1987 Boom

The Expert-Systems Boom

XCON (Digital Equipment) auto-configures computer orders — rule-based AI turns a profit. Japan's Fifth Generation Project (1982) alarms Western governments; DARPA answers with the Strategic Computing Initiative (1983). By the mid-1980s the expert-systems industry is worth over $1 billion a year.

1987 – 2000

The Second AI Winter & Recovery

1987 – 1993 Second AI Winter

The expert systems collapse

Expert systems proved expensive, rigid and hard to maintain. Overhype plus funding cuts stalled the field again, and Japan's Fifth Generation project fell short (1992). Yet research quietly advanced neural networks and statistical learning in the background.

1990s – 2000s Recovery

The web makes data — and Deep Blue wins

Tim Berners-Lee's World Wide Web (1990–91) creates massive digital datasets. AI shifts from rules to data-driven ML — decision trees, Bayesian nets, statistics. Support Vector Machines (1995) sharpen handwriting and image recognition, and in 1997 IBM's Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov.

2000 – 2012

The Foundations of Modern AI

2006 Infrastructure

Big Data & cloud computing

The internet and social media generate enormous data. Amazon Web Services (2006) makes powerful computing rentable, and cheap GPUs join Big Data to supercharge research.

2012 Revolution

The Deep-Learning revolution

AlexNet (Hinton, Krizhevsky, Sutskever) wins the ImageNet challenge by a wide margin, proving deep neural nets beat traditional methods — and triggering huge investment from Google, Microsoft, Meta and Amazon.

The turning point Synthesis

Four forces converge

By 2012, Big Data + cloud + GPUs + deep learning turned AI from a research field into practical technology — the foundation for today's generative AI.

2012 – 2017

The Deep-Learning Breakthrough

2012 Vision

AlexNet cracks ImageNet

An 8-layer CNN sorts 1.2M images into 1,000 categories, cutting top-5 error from 26.2% → 15.3%.

2014 Generation

GANs & DeepFace

AI learns to create, not just recognise — and reaches near-human accuracy at recognising faces.

2016 Mastery

AlphaGo beats Lee Sedol

It masters Go, long thought AI-proof, and moves past human knowledge with faster-than-human intuition.

2017 Architecture

The Transformer

"Self-attention" lets every word see every other word at once — parallel, book-length context. The architecture behind all major AI models that follow.

2018 – 2022

The Rise of Large Language Models

2018 Paradigm

BERT & GPT-1

Pretrain a transformer on huge unlabelled text, then fine-tune cheaply — "pretrain big, adapt cheap" becomes the paradigm.

2020 Scale

GPT-3 — 175B parameters

Ten times bigger, it shows emergent few-shot learning — give it a few examples and it performs the task.

2020 – 2021 Science

AlphaFold2

Solves the 50-year protein-folding problem at 92.4 GDT — leading to a 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

2021 Tooling

GitHub Copilot

AI coding assistance goes mainstream — the LLM becomes a daily tool.

2022 Inflection

ChatGPT

1M users in 5 days, 100M in 2 months — the single biggest moment in AI history.

2023 – 2026

The Age of Agentic AI

2023 Rivalry

GPT-4, Claude & Gemini

AI shifts from monopoly to rivalry — and the scale of investment surges.

2024 Reasoning

o1 reasoning & Sora

Models that "think before answering" plus text-to-video go mainstream; the EU AI Act brings the first major AI regulation.

2025 Efficiency

DeepSeek R1

Matches o1 on many benchmarks at ~95% lower cost — and markets panic.

2025 – 2026 Agentic

Agentic AI goes mainstream

Claude, GPT-5.x and Gemini 3 plan multi-step tasks in real production — Salesforce alone reports $800M in agent revenue.
