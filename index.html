01 — Professional Bio

Who I am

I'm a DevOps engineer based in Atlanta and a graduate student in Artificial Intelligence at Indiana Wesleyan University. Over the past five-plus years I've specialized in building and operating cloud infrastructure — Kubernetes, Helm, and Terraform across AWS and Azure — with an emphasis on doing large-scale change safely.

That work has included migrating 100+ production services from Helm 2 to Helm 3, performing blue-green upgrades across 109 EC2 instances with zero downtime, and cutting Azure AKS costs by 50% through Terraform-driven optimization. The throughline is systems thinking: understanding how the pieces connect so that reliability, cost, and observability improve together rather than in isolation.

Lately I've been focused on where dependable engineering meets AI — designing tools that are not just capable but usable and trustworthy. That interest drives my graduate studies and Imvlar, an audio-first AI learning tool I'm building that reads technical content aloud and answers questions using personalized analogies. My goal is to keep making complex, expert-level knowledge accessible to the people who need it most.

Personal Value Proposition
As a DevOps engineer with a growing focus on applied AI, I turn fragile, expensive infrastructure into systems that are reliable, observable, and cost-efficient at scale — and I bring that same systems thinking to AI, designing practical tools with the guardrails and judgment that keep them trustworthy for the people who use them.

ARTIFACT 01

Recycle/Reuse Advisor

LIVE · OPEN THE BOT

A conversational AI assistant that helps everyday users decide the best way to handle any item of waste — recycle, reuse, drop-off, or trash — while reinforcing the Reduce > Reuse > Recycle priority. Built as the capstone of an AI Lab exploring how large-language-model tools can make expert knowledge accessible to non-experts.

Objective
Give people a fast, reliable answer to a genuinely confusing everyday question — "how do I dispose of this?" — and quietly teach the reduce-reuse-recycle hierarchy in the process.
Tools
ChatbaseGPT-class LLMPrompt engineeringDesign Thinking
Process
Built with the Design Thinking cycle (Empathy → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test) and grounded in research across three AI tools. Two decisions shaped it: when I found the platform could not accept end-user photo uploads, I adapted the design so the bot reasons from a user's spoken description instead; and I rewrote its instructions from a rigid decision tree into a principle-based prompt, letting the model reason freely within guardrails rather than following a brittle script. It was then tested against eight scenarios and deployed as a public, no-login page.
Artifact-Specific Value Proposition
This artifact shows I can scope a broad, research-heavy problem into a focused, usable AI tool — and that I design for trust, not just capability: building guardrails that stop confident-but-wrong answers and adapting the build around real platform constraints. For a team adopting AI, that's the difference between a demo and something you can actually put in front of users.
ARTIFACT 02

Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning

OPEN · APPLICATION ANALYSIS

A decision-focused analysis of when machine learning is the right tool and when deep learning is — worked through two real applications: stock beta estimation with linear regression, and automated document extraction with intelligent document processing. Reframed from a written report into a scannable, side-by-side comparison with a decision matrix at its core.

Objective
Show that choosing between ML and deep learning isn't about which is more advanced — it's about the shape of the problem: the data, the relationship, the need for interpretability, and how much variety the system has to handle.
Tools
Linear regressionCNN + NLPCAPM / betaDocument AIInformation design
Process
I paired one machine-learning case (beta estimation, where data is structured and interpretability is non-negotiable) against one deep-learning case (document extraction, where input is raw and endlessly variable), and argued each way — why the chosen method fits and why the other fails. I distilled the written report into a six-question decision matrix and matched fit / doesn't-fit panels, so the reasoning reads at a glance rather than paragraph by paragraph.
Artifact-Specific Value Proposition
This artifact shows I can reason about AI systems at the level of trade-offs, not just tools — knowing why a simple, explainable model beats a neural network in finance, and why only deep learning survives the messiness of real documents. That judgment is what keeps AI projects from over-engineering the easy problems and under-powering the hard ones.
ARTIFACT 03

The Evolution of Artificial Intelligence

OPEN · INTERACTIVE TIMELINE

An analysis retracing 76 years of AI — 1950 to 2026, from Alan Turing's Imitation Game to today's agentic systems. Built as an animated, scrollable web timeline that visualizes the field's two "AI winters" against its springs of breakthrough, so the boom-and-bust rhythm of the history reads at a glance.

Objective
Turn a dense span of AI history into something a reader actually wants to move through — telling the story of how machines learned to think without losing the rigor behind it.
Tools
HTML / CSS / JSCanvas animationData storytellingDesign systemResearch
Process
I researched the history across nine eras, then designed it as an interactive artifact. Two decisions carried it: a cool/warm color code that marks the AI winters against periods of progress — encoding a real pattern in the history rather than decorating it — and a scroll-driven timeline with a self-drawing spine and a subtle neural-network hero animation, all in a single dependency-free page that reuses this portfolio's design system.
Artifact-Specific Value Proposition
This artifact shows I can take information-heavy material and make it clear and compelling — closing the gap between research and communication. It's the same instinct behind good infrastructure work: the value isn't only in the content, it's in presenting a complex system so the people who need it can actually follow it.
